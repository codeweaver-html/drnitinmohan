FAQs for Parents

Practical answers about children’s digestive health, endoscopy, investigations and clinic visits.

Information for parents

Clear answers to common questions about children’s digestive health, investigations and visiting the clinic. For a more detailed guide, including endoscopy and colonoscopy, visit the full FAQ page.

ConsultationTests and proceduresVisiting the clinic

Consultation

What concerns can I discuss with a paediatric gastroenterologist?

Persistent abdominal pain, vomiting, constipation, diarrhoea, feeding difficulties, poor weight gain and liver-related concerns can be discussed. The consultation helps determine whether further assessment or tests are needed.

What should I bring to my child’s first visit?

Bring previous prescriptions, blood tests, imaging and endoscopy reports, discharge summaries, a list of medicines and recent height and weight records if available. A short note about the symptoms and when they occur can also be helpful.

Tests and procedures

Will my child need an endoscopy?

Not necessarily. Endoscopy is considered only when the child’s symptoms, examination and previous results suggest that it may provide useful information. Many children can be assessed without an invasive procedure. The doctor will discuss the reason for any proposed test.

Is endoscopy safe for children?

Paediatric endoscopy is generally considered safe when there is a clear medical reason and it is performed by a trained team with appropriate monitoring. As with any procedure, there are potential risks, including those related to sedation or anaesthesia. The team will explain the benefits, individual risks and preparation before proceeding.

This information is general and does not replace an individual medical assessment. For urgent or rapidly worsening symptoms, seek prompt in-person medical care.
