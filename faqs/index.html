Consultation preparation

What should I bring to my child’s consultation?

Bring any previous prescriptions, blood test results, ultrasound or other imaging reports, endoscopy reports, discharge summaries and a list of medicines your child is taking. If available, bring growth records such as height and weight measurements. A short note about the symptoms, when they started, food or medicines that affect them, bowel habits and your child’s usual diet can also help the doctor understand the concern.

Should I bring my child’s previous medical records?

Yes. Previous prescriptions, laboratory reports, ultrasound or other imaging, endoscopy reports and hospital discharge summaries can help the doctor understand what has already been investigated or treated. Bring the original reports or clear copies if you have them.

Should I note my child’s symptoms before the visit?

It can be helpful to note when the symptoms began, how often they occur, whether they are related to meals, and whether there are changes in stools, appetite, weight or growth. Also note current medicines and any relevant food or medicine triggers. This information can make the consultation more focused.

Conditions and specialist focus

What concerns does paediatric gastroenterology cover?

Paediatric gastroenterology focuses on digestive system concerns in infants, children and adolescents. The concerns listed within the KidsGastro practice include abdominal pain, vomiting, constipation, diarrhoea and other gastrointestinal symptoms. The cause of a symptom can vary, so assessment depends on the child’s history, examination and any relevant reports.

Does the practice include paediatric liver concerns?

Yes. Paediatric hepatology is part of the listed clinical focus. It relates to assessment of liver-related concerns in children, including jaundice and abnormal liver function test results. The need for blood tests, imaging or further assessment depends on the individual child’s clinical findings.

Does the practice include pancreatic conditions?

Yes. Pancreatology and pancreatic investigations are included among the listed areas of focus and services. Children with suspected pancreatic or related digestive concerns require an individual clinical assessment to decide whether further tests are appropriate.

Are biliary tract conditions included?

Yes. The listed clinical focus includes biliary tract diseases and investigations. The biliary system includes the gallbladder and bile ducts, which carry bile from the liver. The appropriate evaluation depends on the child’s symptoms, examination and investigation results.

Can feeding, nutrition or growth concerns be discussed?

Yes. Paediatric nutrition and growth assessment are listed areas of service. Parents can discuss feeding difficulties, nutritional concerns and poor weight gain. Bringing your child’s recent height and weight records, dietary details and any previous growth chart can help with the assessment.

When should parents consider a specialist consultation?

Consider seeking medical advice if a child has persistent or recurrent abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, ongoing diarrhoea or constipation, blood in the stool, poor weight gain, prolonged jaundice, pale stools or unexplained abnormal liver function tests. These symptoms can have different causes and do not by themselves establish a diagnosis. A clinician can assess whether specialist review or investigations are needed.

Investigations and services

What types of services are listed on the website?

The listed service areas include paediatric gastrointestinal procedures and diagnostic investigations, hepatology procedures and investigations, pancreatic investigations, biliary tract investigations, gastrointestinal diagnostic investigations, and nutrition and growth assessment. The specific service recommended depends on the child’s clinical needs.

Are endoscopy and colonoscopy among the investigations discussed?

Yes. The investigations described on the website include upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, colonoscopy, flexible sigmoidoscopy and gastrointestinal manometry. These are specialised tests and are considered only when clinically indicated. The doctor will discuss the reason for a test, its potential benefits and relevant preparation with the family.

Will every child need an investigation or procedure?

No. A consultation does not automatically mean that a child will need an endoscopy, colonoscopy or another procedure. The decision depends on the symptoms, medical history, examination and any previous test results. Investigations should be undertaken only when the treating clinician considers them appropriate.

Are all investigations and procedures available at the hospital?

Availability may depend on the specific investigation, the child’s age, clinical requirements and current hospital arrangements. Please contact the clinic or hospital in advance to confirm whether a particular test or procedure can be arranged and whether any preparation is required.

General information and safety

Does this FAQ replace a medical consultation?

No. This page provides general information about the listed paediatric gastroenterology services and practical appointment questions. It cannot assess an individual child, provide a diagnosis or replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional. Decisions about tests and treatment should be made after an appropriate clinical assessment.

What should I do if my child has severe or rapidly worsening symptoms?

Seek prompt medical assessment from an appropriate emergency or urgent-care service if your child is severely unwell or has rapidly worsening symptoms. Do not wait for an online reply or a routine appointment if urgent care may be needed. This FAQ is informational and is not an emergency service.
